In practical engineering applications, key structural components are routinely subjected to complex non-proportional variable amplitude loading spectra that severely degrade the material’s fatigue life compared to the simple uniaxial conditions. The additional material response arising from the stochastic random loading significantly challenges the predictive efficacy of current cyclic plasticity constitutive model. To address this critical gap, the present study systematically investigates the cyclic deformation behaviors of AISI 304 stainless steel under constant amplitude, two-stage amplitude, and random amplitude fatigue loading conditions. A robust cyclic plasticity constitutive model is established, incorporating a dynamic plastic strain memory surface linked directly to evolving additional hardening response. Three non-proportional path parameters, comprising a newly proposed instantaneous factor and two classic history-dependent factors, are successfully integrated into a finite element analysis framework. The performance and predictive fidelity of each path factor are comprehensively quantified against experimental datasets. The newly proposed instantaneous path factor delivers the optimal predictive capacity, providing a precise boundary that closely tracks the multiaxial stress responses. Conversely, the history-dependent path factor proposed by Tanaka tends to be overly conservative by slightly overestimating the stress response, while the path factor proposed by Itoh et al. remains unconservative and underestimates the boundary. Furthermore, systematic transmission electron microscopy (TEM) observations reveal a clear physical correlation between macroscopic transient responses and their microstructural drivers. Loading paths with high non-proportionality culminate in the formation of refined dislocation cells or labyrinth substructures that act as formidable barriers to plastic flow. In contrast, the low non-proportionality path primarily exhibits oriented dislocation walls governed by a planar slip mechanism.

ABSTRACT This study comprehensively investigates the damage evolution and property degradation of SiC f /Si 3 N 4 composites subjected to high‐speed waterjet impacts simulating rain erosion. The composites are fabricated by CVI using SiC fiber fabric preform with BN interface and Si 3 N 4 matrix. A single‐jet device generates stable waterjets (≈350 m/s, ≈4.9 mm diameter) to induce controlled erosion damage. Results demonstrate a staged progression of erosion damage: initial localized fiber bundle fracture and matrix cracking (0%–25% depth) is followed by pit widening via water‐wedging and delamination (25%–75% depth), culminating in penetration with a stabilized damage area (>75% depth). Tensile strength decreases by 47.5% after full penetration, with AE analysis indicating a transition from synergistic fiber‐matrix failure to a fiber‐dominated quasi‐brittle fracture mechanism due to interfacial weakening and matrix damage. Conversely, electromagnetic analysis shows that the erosion‐induced damage, including pits, cracks, and increased porosity, improved impedance matching. This leads to a significant enhancement in wave‐absorbing performance, with the minimum reflection loss decreasing from −5.96 dB for undamaged material to below −7 dB for fully eroded specimen across the X‐band. This work elucidates the dual influences of rain erosion on the mechanical and electromagnetic functionality of SiC f /Si 3 N 4 composites, providing critical insights for aerospace applications.)
