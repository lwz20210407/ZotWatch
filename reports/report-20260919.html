High-order isogeometric phase-field fracture modeling: a numerical-experimental study (2026-09-08)
高阶等几何相场断裂建模：数值-实验研究
Luigi Greco, Christopher Benz, Alessandro Reali, Lars Radtke
研究EN AW-6060铝合金非对齐预裂纹试样中高阶相场断裂模型的预测能力
Abstract The synergy between laboratory testing and numerical analysis represents a key frontier in the field of fracture mechanics. In this work, we investigate a numerical–experimental comparison for a dog-bone specimen featuring non-aligned pre-notches, made of an EN AW-6060 aluminum alloy exhibiting ductile fracture behavior. The central idea of this study is to demonstrate that a brittle fracture phase-field model, specifically, a fourth-order AT2 formulation, can provide physically meaningful and conservative predictions when applied to a ductile fracture problem, without explicitly accounting for plastic dissipation. The study aims to achieve both qualitative (crack pattern) and quantitative (peak reaction force) agreement between experiments and simulations, while assessing the influence of key phase-field parameters, including fracture toughness and internal length, on the overall response. The results show that the brittle model yields peak load predictions within 3.5% of the experimental measurements and successfully reproduces the experimentally observed crack patterns, supporting its use as a conservative design tool in engineering applications

Leveraging machine learning for robust parameter identification of Yld2004-18p (2026-08)

通过单孔扩张实验和ANN框架识别AA6022-T4的各向异性并校准Yld2004-18p屈服函数
This study proposes an experiment-efficient ANN-based framework for characterizing the anisotropy of AA6022-T4 and calibrating the Yld2004-18p anisotropic yield function from a single hole expansion (HE) test and evaluates its predictions against two texture-driven meso-scale plasticity approaches, namely crystal plasticity finite element method (CPFEM) and elasto-visco-plastic self-consistent (ΔEVPSC). The ANN framework exploits the heterogeneous deformation field generated during the HE test, with training data obtained from finite element (FE) simulations using randomly assigned Yld2004-18p parameters. Two distinct ANN frameworks are developed: one predicts anisotropic properties (stress ratios and r-values), while the other directly estimates Yld2004-18p parameters. Three ANN input selection strategies are explored, i.e., using all strain components from 21 extraction locations in the HE simulation, local stress-based selection, and a variance-based approach. In the crystal plasticity approaches, CPFEM and ΔEVPSC simulations based solely on the measured initial crystallographic texture provide virtual uniaxial and equibiaxial responses that are subsequently fitted to Yld2004-18p. Comparative assessment shows that the ANN framework, as well as the CPFEM- and ΔEVPSC-based approaches, reproduces the anisotropic response, yield locus, and HE thinning profile of the material with generally good agreement with experiments and with the conventional calibration, while substantially reducing experimental time, cost, and material consumption.

07 塑性变形与硬化律
Identification of Combined Isotropic–Kinematic Hardening Parameters from Reverse Bending Tests Using Recurrent Neural Networks (2026-09-16)
利用循环神经网络从反向弯曲试验中识别组合等向-随动强化参数
Daniel J. Cruz, Rui L. Amaral, Manuel R. Barbosa, Abel D. Santos, Jose Cesar de Sa
Metals
通过反向弯曲试验和Bi-LSTM网络实现组合等向-随动强化参数的快速识别
Accurate identification of constitutive parameters is essential for reliable finite element simulations of sheet metal forming processes. Conventional inverse identification techniques, such as Finite Element Model Updating (FEMU), generally require iterative optimization procedures involving numerous finite element simulations, resulting in high computational cost. This work proposes a machine learning framework based on a bidirectional Long Short-Term Memory (Bi-LSTM) neural network for the identification of combined isotropic–kinematic hardening parameters from a custom-designed reverse bending test. A synthetic dataset comprising 5000 finite element simulations was generated by systematically varying the parameters of the Swift isotropic hardening law and the Armstrong–Frederick kinematic hardening law using Sobol sampling. The resulting force–displacement histories were used to train the Bi-LSTM network to predict five constitutive parameters (K, σ0, n, C, and γ) directly from force–displacement responses. To improve the physical admissibility of the predictions, a physically constrained loss function was introduced by penalizing negative values of the kinematic hardening parameters during training. The proposed model achieved coefficients of determination, R2 above 0.98 for the isotropic and kinematic hardening parameters while reducing the number of physically inadmissible predictions from approximately 30 to a single case. Experimental validation was performed on DP500 and DP780 advanced high-strength steels, where finite element simulations using the Bi-LSTM predicted parameters reproduced the experimentally measured springback angles with good agreement. The proposed methodology provides near-instantaneous constitutive parameter identification after training, eliminating the need for computationally expensive iterative optimization while maintaining high predictive accuracy and physical consistency.

Large-Deformation Phase-Field Modeling of Thrombus Fracture With Fiber Reorientation (2026-09-16)
考虑纤维重排的大变形血栓断裂相场建模
Wu Qiong, Zhang Xiaomin, Zhao Zhipeng, Huang Chentao
Journal of Applied Mechanics
建立大变形下血栓断裂的相场模型，考虑纤维网络各向异性演化，模拟断裂行为。
Abstract Thrombus fragmentation during mechanical thrombectomy may lead to distal embolization, yet its fracture mechanism remains difficult to predict because thrombi undergo large deformation, fiber-network rearrangement, and composition-dependent mechanical responses. This study aims to develop a continuum fracture framework for analyzing thrombus crack initiation and propagation while accounting for evolving fibrin-network anisotropy. A finite-deformation anisotropic phase-field fracture model is formulated by coupling a hyperelastic matrix response, fiber-induced anisotropic reinforcement, composition-dependent constitutive scaling, damage degradation, and a stretch-driven fiber-reorientation law. The main innovation is to treat the preferred fibrin direction as an evolving internal structural variable that reorients toward the local maximum-principal-stretch direction and influences the anisotropic stored energy and crack-driving history field. The model is implemented in abaqus through a staggered user-defined material–user-defined element procedure. The constitutive parameters are calibrated using fibrin-gel tensile data, and the fracture response is compared with notched uniaxial fracture experiments using experimentally motivated fracture toughness values. Additional numerical parametric studies are performed to examine the effects of fiber dispersion, reorientation parameters, and loading configuration on fracture behavior. On the other hand, microvoids were also initiated later due to martensitic cracking after the maximum load was reached, and these microvoids subsequently exhibited rapid growth. The martensite cracking induced additional damage evolution mainly along nearby ferrite/martensite interfaces and intersections between the martensite and the ferrite grain boundary. It is notable that the microvoids originating from martensitic cracking exhibited characteristic shear-dominated growth under macroscopic tension, whereas those originating from the other nucleation sites exhibited traditional triaxiality-dominated growth. It was concluded that the ductile fracture was dominated by the substantial force driving the growth of microvoids located on morphologically characteristic martensitic particles.
