Angle-dependent shielding performance of Ti-6Al-4V/AISI 1045 bilayer panel under hypervelocity impact (2026-10-29)
Ti-6Al-4V/AISI 1045双层板在超高速冲击下的角度依赖性防护性能研究

International Journal of Damage Mechanics
对Ti-6Al-4V/AISI 1045双层板，研究其在超高速铝球冲击下的角度依赖性防护性能，得到能量分布和后表面隆起的影响规律。
Hypervelocity ballistic impacts are becoming common with scientific advancements in propulsion technology. Space collisions like meteoroids also involve high-velocity impacts. Traditional metallic sheets like Ti-6Al-4V and AISI 1045 steel, when employed in thin configurations, exhibit limited capacity to endure the severe stresses generated during high-speed impact events. In this paper, the response of a Ti-6Al-4V titanium alloy and AISI 1045 steel bilayer panel to a hypervelocity aluminum ball impact was studied to observe the underlying phenomenon of spalling, cavitation, and debris-cloud formation caused by such hypervelocity impacts. Using the Modified Johnson–Cook material model along with the Grüneisen equation of state to represent the impacted materials and implementing the smoothed particle hydrodynamics approach within the commercially available finite-element software LS-DYNA, the penetration behavior under symmetric and asymmetric impact conditions was investigated. Results demonstrated that impact angle, after the critical threshold of 30°, strongly influenced the energy distribution, flow stress, and the rear-face bulge formation of the lower AISI 1045 steel plate. These findings provide practical design guidance for optimizing multi-layer metallic shielding and impact-resistant structures against hypervelocity debris.

06 温度效应与热软化
A High-Temperature Semi-Coupled Damage Model for 7075 Aluminum Alloy Considering Temperature and Strain Rate Effects (2026-10-31)
考虑温度和应变率效应的7075铝合金高温半耦合损伤模型研究
Dan Yao, Muyu Li, Zhiyong Chen, Yixu Niu, Bin Li, Dalei Zu, Yingping Guan

对7075铝合金，建立考虑温度和应变率效应的高温半耦合损伤模型，准确预测高温下的损伤起始、演化和断裂行为。
Fracture is a common and fatal defect in the hot stamping of aluminum alloys. Accurate prediction of high-temperature fracture behavior is crucial for forming process planning. However, existing high-temperature fracture criteria suffer from a trade-off between predictive capability and parameter calibration difficulty, which limits their industrial application. This study establishes a high-temperature semi-coupled damage model by incorporating strain rate and temperature terms into a semi-coupled fracture framework and coupling it with a viscoplastic constitutive model. This model maintains the convenience of parameter calibration while describing the nonlinear characteristics of damage evolution using a hyperbolic sine function. The model was applied to the finite element analysis of 7075 aluminum alloy. For hot tensile tests, the simulated load–displacement responses, fracture displacements, and strain fields agree with the experiments, with a maximum load–displacement root mean square error (RMSE) of 0.047 kN and a maximum fracture displacement relative error of 3.141%. For isothermal bulging validation tests, the average relative error for fracture location prediction is 5.10%, and the maximum relative error for normalized thickness is 6.67%. These results demonstrate that within 350–450 °C and 0.001–0.1 s−1, the proposed model accurately predicts the damage initiation, evolution, and fracture behavior.

08 复杂应力状态与屈服准则
Prediction of fracture behavior in cold roll-bonded multilayer metallic composite sheets using ductile fracture criteria (2026-10-30)
利用延性断裂准则预测冷轧叠层金属复合板的断裂行为
Amir Hossein Bakhshi, Hossein Talebi-Ghadikolaee, Mohammad Honarpisheh, Ramin Hashemi
Journal of Materials Research and Technology
对冷轧叠层金属复合板，利用延性断裂准则预测其断裂行为，得到不同材料组合下的断裂预测准确性。
In this study, the fracture behavior of CRB Al/Cu multilayer sheets was investigated using six uncoupled ductile fracture criteria including Ayada, Rice–Tracy, Brozzo, Oh, Oyane–Sato, and Ko–Huh. Five different materials were examined, including pure aluminum (AA1050), pure copper, Al/Al bilayer, Al/Cu bilayer, and Cu/Al/Cu trilayer composites. The calibration of the fracture models was performed using a hybrid approach (experimental tests, theoretical analysis and numerical simulation), and subsequently validated through independent plane-strain tensile tests. The results show that the predictive capability of uncoupled fracture criteria is strongly material-dependent. The Brozzo criterion provides the highest accuracy for the Al/Al bilayer (1.6% error), the Oh criterion performs best for the Al/Cu bilayer (0.1% error), and the Ko–Huh criterion gives the closest prediction for the Cu/Al/Cu trilayer (1.8% error). A comparison of the calibrated fracture loci further reveals that prediction accuracy is governed by the compatibility between the stress-state sensitivity of the fracture criterion and the damage evolution of the multilayer architecture, rather than by the intrinsic superiority of any individual model. These findings demonstrate that no single fracture criterion is universally applicable to CRB multilayer sheets. Nevertheless, the Ko–Huh and Oyane–Sato criteria exhibited relatively consistent performance across the investigated multilayer configurations, indicating that they can be considered suitable candidates for fracture prediction of the investigated multilayer sheets within the stress-state range examined in this study.

Chemical short-range order as a conditional modifier of deformation and damage in void-containing high-entropy alloys under high-strain-rate loading (2026-11-01)
化学短程有序作为含孔高熵合金在高应变率加载下变形与损伤的条件修饰因子
Lei Wu, Haonan Sui, Qi Zhu, An-Min He, Heng-An Wu, Pei Wang
Materials & Design
研究含孔高熵合金Al 0.3 FeCoCrNi在高应变率加载下的拉伸响应，发现化学短程有序对损伤演化具有条件性修饰作用。
Chemical short-range order (SRO) can markedly affect the mechanical response of high-entropy alloys (HEAs), but its role in void-containing alloys under high-strain-rate loading remains unclear. Here, molecular dynamics simulations and a data-driven surrogate model were combined to investigate the tensile response of Al 0.3 FeCoCrNi HEAs with pre-existing nanoscale voids. The results reveal a coupling effect between structural defects and local chemical ordering. The size of pre-existing voids dominates yield onset, peak strength and damage evolution by intensifying stress concentration and promoting earlier plasticity. By contrast, SRO acts as a conditional atomic-scale modifier. For smaller voids, SRO improves tensile resistance by reducing active dislocation nucleation sites, confining early plastic zones, and promoting sessile reactions including Lomer-Cottrell locks and Hirth dislocations. This effect becomes less pronounced for larger voids, where deformation is increasingly governed by void-induced stress concentration. Temperature further constrains the SRO effect: SRO promotes twinning and organized planar-defect evolution at 300 K, whereas thermal activation suppresses twinning and causes diffuse post-yield defect accumulation at 1200 K. Application-oriented simulations, such as automotive crash simulations, often utilize large shell elements to optimize computational efficiency. This study applies the crack band approach typically used for concrete materials to steel sheets. The aim was to examine this regularization method for different stress states with plane stress shell elements to reduce mesh sensitivity, in respect to element size as well as to element shape. Finite element simulations of four specimens covering the lower and mid triaxiality range were performed with LS-Dyna using small solid elements and large shell elements. For each specimen, the results of a finite element simulation performed with large shell elements were compared with results of a block of solid elements of equal dimensions. Each finite element solver uses different shell formulations, which can influence the exact results of the simulation. Nonetheless, the comparison provides insights into the expected behavior compared to the behavior resulting from shell simulations. The guidelines developed in this study were tested numerically for a tensile test with a spot weld by comparing differently discretized meshes. The results show that the characteristic length determined by the crack band approach improves shell element simulations compared to the conventionally calculated characteristic length. This approach accommodates various aspect ratios and arbitrarily shaped shell elements.

In this article, a coupled experimental and numerical method is utilized for characterizing the elastic–plastic constitutive properties of ductile materials. Three-dimensional digital image correlation (DIC) is used to measure the full field deformation on two mutually orthogonal surfaces of a uniaxial tensile test specimen. The material’s constitutive model, whose parameters are unknown a priori, is determined through an optimization process that compares these experimental measurements with finite element simulations in which the constitutive model is implemented. The optimization procedure utilizes the robust dataset of locally observed deformation measurements from DIC in addition to the standard measurements of boundary load and displacement data. When the difference between the experiment and simulations is reduced sufficiently, a set of parameters is found for the material model that is suitable to large strain levels. This method proves to be a powerful tool for calibration of material models. The final parameters produce a simulation that tracks the local experimental displacement field to within a couple percent of error. Simultaneously, the percent error in the simulation for the load carried by the specimen throughout the test is less than 1%. Additionally, half of the parameters for Hill’s yield criterion, describing anisotropy of the normal stresses, are found from a single tensile test. This method will find even greater utility in calibrating more complex material models by greatly reducing the experimental effort required to identify the appropriate model parameters.
