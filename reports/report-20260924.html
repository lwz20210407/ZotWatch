Journal of Manufacturing and Materials Processing
研究AA3105铝合金板在柔性辊弯成型过程中的断裂机制，提出减少损伤的方法。
Fracture is one of the most common defects that occur during the roll forming process. However, several process-based strategies can be employed to minimize its occurrence. This paper investigates the fracture mechanisms of AA3105 aluminum alloy sheets during the flexible roll forming (FRF) process, focusing on methods to reduce damage and prevent failure throughout forming. Experimental tests were carried out to determine the mechanical properties of the sheet and to calibrate the modified Mohr–Coulomb fracture criterion. The digital image correlation technique was used to measure the experimental strain field at the onset of fracture. Finite element simulations of the calibration tests were then performed to validate the failure criterion, showing good agreement with experimental results. After calibration, the FRF process was simulated in ABAQUS, and a user subroutine was implemented to predict fracture evolution during forming. The simulation results were validated against experimental data obtained from the FRF machine, confirming the model’s accuracy. The results revealed that increasing the sheet thickness from 0.5 mm to 1.5 mm increased the damage at the critical element by approximately 52%, whereas increasing the transition radius from 250 mm to 550 mm and the bending radius from 1 mm to 3 mm reduced the damage by approximately 28% and 13%, respectively. In addition, increasing the number of forming stands reduced the damage by approximately 10%. These findings demonstrate that both thinner sheets and multi-stage forming significantly enhance formability and reduce fracture risk in FRF of aluminum sheets.

: Accurate modeling of plasticity plays an important role in improving manufacturing efficiency and product quality. Currently, several asymmetric yield criteria have been developed, but most of these criteria have limitations, such as insufficient capability to capture the yield behavior under shear and near-plane strain tension. To overcome these limitations and accurately model both the yield behavior of metal sheets under various stress states and the anisotropic responses under uniaxial tension and compression, a comprehensive framework that combines an even function of stress states with an anisotropic-asymmetric yield function is developed. Within this framework, an extended analytical Yoon2014 criterion is proposed. The extended criterion is employed to describe the initial yield loci of AZ31, AA5754, AA2A12, and DP980 sheets, as well as the evolving yield loci of AA2A12 and QP1180 sheets. Furthermore, the extended criterion along with a Hill48 plastic potential is used in numerical simulations to predict the plastic behavior of A5052 sheets in a cylindrical cup drawing test. The results obtained from the extended model show good agreement with the experimental data. Applications to the five investigated sheets show that the proposed criterion can effectively characterize both the initial yield behavior and hardening responses of diverse metal sheets under uniaxial tension and compression along different directions, equibiaxial tension and equibiaxial compression (estimated empirically), as well as near-plane strain tension and shear. The cup drawing simulations further demonstrate that the proposed model combined with an appropriate plastic potential can effectively predict the plastic behavior of metal sheets in forming processes.

Constitutive Modeling Method for AM 316 Based on Johnson-Cook Guided PCA-MLP Residual Learning (2026-09-23)

zequn lv, an he, jining li, yihang fan, hang zhao, yongshuo xu, wenchang fu, yingbo wang 等 9 人
基于Johnson-Cook模型的PCA-MLP残差学习方法，用于准确预测AM 316不锈钢在高温和高应变率下的动态流动应力。
Abstract Accurate prediction of dynamic flow stress for additively manufactured (AM) 316L stainless steel under high temperatures and high strain rates is critical for service safety evaluation and forming process optimization. To overcome limited accuracy of conventional empirical constitutive models and weak generalization of purely data-driven models under small datasets, this work develops a Johnson–Cook guided PCA-MLP residual learning model (JC-PCA-MLP). Taking the Johnson–Cook model as the physical baseline, principal component analysis (PCA) reduces dimensions of residual stress between experimental measurements and JC predictions. A multilayer perceptron (MLP) predicts PCA coefficients of residuals to reconstruct full stress–strain curves. Validated on dynamic compression data of AM 316L stainless steel across various temperatures and high strain rates, unseen intermediate temperatures (272°C and 450°C) are used for testing. The model achieves average R² of 0.9306 and 0.9538, average absolute relative errors (AARE) of 1.97% and 1.63%, and minimum R² of 0.8779 and 0.8909 at 272°C and 450°C, respectively. Compared with the JC, MLP and PCA-MLP models, JC-PCA-MLP delivers better prediction accuracy and stability. This work offers an efficient physics-informed data-driven strategy for dynamic constitutive modeling of additively manufactured 316L stainless steel.

Transmission casings may undergo time-dependent deformation under sustained thermal and mechanical loading, which cannot be fully characterized by conventional static-strength checks. To evaluate the relative service performance of six AZ80-series magnesium-alloy states in a casing, this study integrates experimental and published data and establishes a unified workflow spanning creep data, constitutive calibration, standard-specimen finite element response, and equivalent-casing assessment. A common transient-steady creep equation is used to describe all six material states, and the constitutive relations calibrated at specimen level are applied directly to the same equivalent casing model. The component responses are compared under consistent computational conditions and assessment criteria. As temperature increases, the casing response shifts from instantaneous load-bearing control toward time-dependent deformation control. Within the 100 h analysis window, C, F, R, and RT5 reach the 5% total-strain threshold under some elevated-temperature conditions, whereas R175T6 and R250T6 remain below this threshold. At 175°C, their maximum total strains are 2.97% and 2.55%, respectively, indicating lower creep-deformation accumulation and better relative deformation stability. From the perspective of creep-deformation control, R175T6 and R250T6 can therefore be considered candidate states for subsequent high-temperature casing assessment. The creep model primarily describes the primary and secondary stages and does not address tertiary-stage damage or creep-rupture life. A complete high-temperature safety assessment of the two T6 states requires additional strength and ductility data.
