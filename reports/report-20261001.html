Inverse Thermal Softening Behavior in a High‐Entropy Alloy Under Various Strain Rates (2026-09-28)
高熵合金在不同应变率下的逆热软化行为
Zhi Wang, Jianjun Wang, Qiang Wang, Qiang Wang, Zhiming Jiao, Dan Zhao, Zhihua Wang, Zhihua Wang 等 9 人
Rare Metals
研究CoCrFeNiTi 0.2高熵合金在极端热机械耦合环境下的变形机制，发现其在不同应变率下表现出逆热软化行为。
ABSTRACT Although face‐centered cubic high‐entropy alloys (HEAs) have received extensive attention and in‐depth research, most existing studies focus on single temperature or strain rate conditions, lacking systematic investigations into deformation mechanisms under extreme thermomechanical coupling environments. In this work, a CoCrFeNiTi 0.2 HEA containing ultrafine L1 2 precipitates and η phases was fabricated, and compression tests were performed over a wide range of temperatures (293–1273 K) and strain rates (0.001–5000 s −1 ), followed by systematic characterization of the corresponding microstructural evolution. The results demonstrate an inverse thermal softening behavior at various strain rates. Anomalous double‐humped stress peaks, separately induced by third‐type strain aging and in situ coarsening of L1 2 precipitates during deformation, were observed in the flow stress–temperature curves, leading to the inverse thermal softening behavior. The stress peak associated with third‐type strain aging appears at various strain rates and shifts toward higher temperatures with increasing strain rate. Ti atoms serve as solute atoms, forming solute atmospheres and effectively pinning mobile dislocations. In contrast, the other stress peak induced by in situ coarsening of L1 2 precipitates is nearly insensitive to strain rate. The dependence of multiple deformation mechanisms—including dislocation slip, twinning, stacking fault‐mediated plasticity, and dynamic recrystallization—on temperature and strain rate was thoroughly investigated. Finally, a schematic diagram of deformation mechanisms covering the entire investigated temperature and strain rate range was constructed. This study elucidates the deformation mechanisms of the CoCrFeNiTi 0.2 HEA in extreme environments, offering an important theoretical basis and experimental support for the design and application of high‐performance HEAs in aerospace, weaponry, and other fields.

Modification of the Johnson-Cook constitutive model for 34CrNi3MoA alloy steel based on the temperature-strain rate coupling effect (2026-09-30)
基于温度-应变率耦合效应的34CrNi3MoA合金钢Johnson-Cook本构模型修正
Jie Shen
Twelfth International Conference on Mechanical Engineering, Materials, and Automation Technology (MMEAT 2026)
通过SHPB试验研究34CrNi3MoA合金钢在不同温度和应变率下的高应变率压缩响应，修正Johnson-Cook模型以提高预测精度。
This work examines the high-rate compression response of 34CrNi3MoA alloy steel by means of SHPB tests carried out at 25 °C, 200 °C, 400 °C and 600 °C, with strain rates ranging from 1000 to 4600 s⁻¹. The experimental curves indicate rate strengthening, thermal softening and a local stress increase related to dynamic strain aging in a specific temperature rate interval. When the conventional Johnson-Cook model was calibrated with the same data set, the average relative error reached 20.63%, showing that the separable strain, rate and temperature terms were insufficient for this material. To improve the description, the model was revised by introducing a rate-dependent thermal-softening exponent, a two dimensional Gaussian function for the dynamic-strain-aging peak and a hardening coefficient coupled with both temperature and strain rate. The revised model reduced the average relative error to 2.88% and gave a closer representation of the measured curves within the tested range. The proposed equation is therefore recommended as an interpolation model for 1000-4600 s⁻¹ and 25-600 °C; application beyond this domain still requires further calibration and uncertainty evaluation.

Physics-informed residual deep learning for constitutive modeling of hot deformation and dynamic recrystallization in a Mo-rich α + β titanium alloy (2026-09-23)
基于物理信息残差深度学习的Mo-rich α + β钛合金热变形与动态再结晶本构建模
Prashil S. Joshi, Diksha Mahadule, Rajesh Kisni Khatirkar
Journal of Alloys and Compounds
利用物理信息残差深度学习模型预测Mo-rich α + β钛合金在热变形和动态再结晶过程中的本构行为。
Accurate constitutive modeling of hot deformation characteristics is essential for the development of thermomechanical procedures for advanced structural alloys. Conventional Arrhenius-type and empirical models frequently find it challenging to effectively depict the combined influences of strain hardening, dynamic recovery (DRV), and dynamic recrystallization (DRX) across extensive processing intervals. This research uses two Stacked Residual Physics-Informed Neural Networks (STAR-PINNs) to predict the hot-deformation characteristics of a Mo-rich α + β titanium alloy (Ti–6Al–4Mo–1V–0.1Si). The Improved STAR-PINN integrates thermomechanical constitutive restrictions, while the DRX-Aware STAR-PINN utilizes a dual-output framework that incorporates recrystallization kinetics through a trainable JMAK–Avrami model. Both models utilize a common residual encoder that is trained on empirical flow-stress data collected at temperatures ranging from 800 to 1050 °C and strain rates between 0.01 and 10 s⁻¹ . Constraints informed by physics that enforce thermal softening, strain-rate sensitivity, strain hardening, and post-peak softening are applied via automatic differentiation. The DRX-Aware model also includes JMAK–Avrami regularization featuring learnable, constrained kinetic parameters k and n, DRX saturation limits, Arrhenius-based consistency, and latent-feature integration connecting the estimated DRX fraction to the stress output. The DRX-Aware STAR-PINN obtained RMSE = 11.62 MPa, MAE = 4.73 MPa, and R² = 0.9852 on the test set reserved for validation, along with a five-fold cross-validated RMSE of 12.74 ± 0.38 MPa. The learned JMAK parameters converged to k = 2.03 and n = 1.99 , remaining close to the conventional values while being inferred directly from the data. The model effectively mirrored flow curves influenced by temperature, DRX development, and Zener–Hollomon correlations under interpolation scenarios while preserving physically coherent constitutive behavior. These findings illustrate that physics-informed deep learning delivers a precise and understandable structure for constitutive modeling, presenting a viable approach for sophisticated process modeling of hot-deformed titanium alloys.

Efficient GA–BP-assisted calibration of a semi-coupled damage framework for thin QP980 steel sheets using limited experimental data (2026-09-04)
基于有限实验数据的半耦合损伤框架高效GA–BP校准方法用于薄QP980钢片
王和源, Yue Zhang, Yongchuan Duan, Yingping Guan, Fangfang Zhang
Materials & Design
利用遗传算法–反向传播神经网络对薄QP980钢片的半耦合损伤框架进行高效校准，提高损伤演化参数预测精度。
Efficient calibration of damage-evolution parameters remains a key challenge in semi-coupled fracture modeling of advanced high-strength steels because these parameters are not directly measurable and usually require repeated experiment–simulation iterations. This study develops a semi-coupled damage framework for thin QP980 steel sheets under limited experimental data. The plastic response is described by the Hill48 yield criterion and a modified Swift–Voce hardening law, damage initiation by a Modified Mohr–Coulomb (MMC)-based fracture locus, and post-initiation softening by a continuum damage mechanics (CDM)-based evolution law. A hierarchical calibration strategy is adopted: plasticity and damage-initiation parameters are determined through conventional experiment–simulation procedures, whereas damage-evolution parameters are identified using a genetic algorithm–backpropagation (GA-BP) neural network. A finite element database was constructed from the R5 notched specimen, with parameter ranges selected to cover the observed softening response. The calibrated framework was validated using notched and shear specimens. The GA–BP-calibrated model reproduced experimental force–displacement curves and fracture displacements, with fracture displacement errors within 3.4%. Digital image correlation (DIC) strain-field comparisons further confirmed the predicted localization patterns. The results provide an efficient, physically constrained route for semi-coupled damage-model calibration and fracture assessment of thin QP980 steel sheets.

Chenglong Duan, Dazhong Wu

通过物理信息机器学习架构预测增材制造材料的应力-应变行为，提高预测性能和物理一致性。
Predicting the stress–strain behaviors of additively manufactured materials is crucial for part qualification in additive manufacturing (AM). Conventional physics-based constitutive models of materials have limitations, such as oversimplification of material properties and narrow applicability to limited strain rates, while purely data-driven machine learning (ML) models often lack physical consistency and interpretability. To address these issues, we introduce a physics-informed machine learning (PIML) framework that leverages physical laws in elastic and plastic regions to improve the predictive performance and physical consistency when predicting the stress–strain curves of additively manufactured polymers and metals. A polynomial regression model is first used to predict the yield point based on AM process parameters, and then the stress–strain curves are segmented into elastic and plastic regions using the predicted yield point. Two long short-term memory (LSTM) models are trained to predict the two regions separately. For the elastic region, Hooke’s law is embedded in the LSTM models for both polymers and metals. For the plastic region, Voce hardening law and Hollomon’s law are embedded in the LSTM models for the polymers and metals, respectively. In addition, the PIML framework is implemented by two architectures, including the loss-based and activation-based PIML architectures, where the physical laws are embedded into the loss and activation functions, respectively. The performance of the two PIML architectures is compared with two LSTM-based ML models, three additional ML models, including a ridge polynomial regression model, an artificial neural network, and a transformer, and a physics-based constitutive model. These models are built on experimental data collected from two additively manufactured polymers (i.e., Nylon and carbon fiber-acrylonitrile butadiene styrene) and two additively manufactured metals (i.e., AlSi10Mg and Ti6Al4V). Experimental results demonstrate that two PIML architectures consistently outperform other ML baseline models and the physics-based constitutive model. The segmental predictive model with the activation-based PIML architecture achieves the best overall performance, with the lowest MAPE of 10.46 ± 0.81 %, the lowest MAE of 26.07 ± 2.52 MPa, the lowest RMSE of 29.28 ± 2.83 MPa, and the highest R 2 of 0.82 ± 0.05, indicating superior predictive performance for four different materials.

Hydrogen-resistant steel is an important engineering material for core components in advanced weapon systems and nuclear physics experiments because of its high strength, high toughness, and excellent resistance to hydrogen embrittlement under high-temperature and high-pressure conditions. In this study, a high-temperature Split Hopkinson pressure bar (SHPB) system was used to characterize the dynamic mechanical response of hydrogen-resistant steel. True stress–strain curves were obtained under different strains, strain rates, and deformation temperatures. The third stress invariant is added to Hill48 criterion to capture the SD effect. Lode angle-dependent enhanced fully coupled damage model is proposed to account for the loading path sensitivity on ductile damage initiation and growth. A detailed material parameters identification procedure is presented. The proposed model is implemented into the FE code Abaqus/Explicit through user defined subroutine VUMAT. The numerically predicted results obtained with the proposed model are compared to experimental data of Ti-6Al-4V and good agreement is observed.
