Automatic-Differentiation-Enabled Finite Element Model Updating: Learning constitutive neural networks from structural deformation data (2026-10-24)
自动微分赋能的有限元模型更新：从结构变形数据学习本构神经网络
Matthias Knipper, Chenyi Ji, Malte Brand, Kevin Linka
Computer Methods in Applied Mechanics and Engineering
通过自动微分技术实现有限元模型更新，从结构变形数据中学习本构神经网络模型。
The identification of constitutive neural network models from heterogeneous full-field deformation data provides a robust alternative to traditional calibration methods based on homogeneous stress–strain experiments, particularly given the high dimensionality of trainable parameters. Existing approaches must balance generality, robustness, and computational efficiency: Conventional finite element model updating is broadly applicable but computationally demanding; weak-form methods offer efficiency but are sensitive to noise and data scarcity; neural operator models are highly expressive but require extensive training datasets. This work presents ADE-FEMU (Automatic-Differentiation-Enabled Finite Element Model Updating), an end-to-end differentiable framework that integrates a constitutive neural network model within a JAX-FEM nonlinear solver and identifies its parameters through gradient-based minimization of a measurement-mismatch loss, allowing for training on structural data with limited kinetic information. Thus, it can be considered as an extension of finite element model updating (FEMU) for hyperelastic materials, implemented within a GPU-accelerated, fully automatically differentiable finite element framework Newton tangent stiffness and loss gradients are computed automatically using forward- and reverse-mode automatic differentiation throughout the entire pipeline, thereby removing the need for analytic adjoints or offline surrogate models. ADE-FEMU is demonstrated for two architectures: a grey-box Constitutive Artificial Neural Network (CANN), a polyconvex, fully connected model with high flexibility, and a white-box CANN, an expert-system network with phenomenologically interpretable strain–energy terms. Focusing on incompressible isotropic hyperelasticity, ADE-FEMU is evaluated on three open experimental datasets: (1) full digital image correlation (DIC) of a perforated tensile specimen, (2) a reduced-data scenario with a one-dimensional stretch profile and global force–displacement curve, and (3) a heterogeneous matrix–inclusion system in which both phases’ constitutive laws are identified and generalized to twenty-two previously unseen samples. The grey-box CANN demonstrates superior performance with comprehensive, full-field, or heterogeneous data, whereas the interpretable white-box CANN is more reliable for model discovery from sparse datasets. The source code will be released upon publication.

Reliable identification of damage-induced property changes in formed metal sheets remains challenging because plastic deformation alters the mechanical response through several coupled mechanisms. Moreover, pronounced ductile damage evolution occurs predominantly under inhomogeneous states of deformation, e.g., strain localization, which compromises the characterization of potential effects of damage on macroscopic mechanical properties, e.g., elastic stiffness. In this study, damage in dual-phase steel DP800 sheets is isolated from other concurrent mechanisms using a novel “sheet extrusion” process. The flat, finitely-strained sheets exhibit large regions with homogeneous properties, enabling subsequent characterization of damage-induced property changes. Damage in the form of microscopic voids is then characterized by large-area scanning electron microscopy (SEM) measurements, and damage-induced property changes are determined by subsequent characterization tests on specimens extracted from the extruded sheets. It is found that deformation-induced damage does not measurably affect the apparent elastic stiffness or the pre-necking tensile response. In contrast, differences in fracture-related performance quantities, i.e., the remaining ductility and the tensile impact energy, indicate that higher deformation-induced damage promotes earlier macroscopic failure by increasing the likelihood of local void coalescence during subsequent loading.

Thin high-strength 7075-T651 aluminum plates are susceptible to radial cracking, petaling, and rear-face fragmentation under ogive-nosed projectile impact. This study investigates the influence of a ductile 2024-T351 front layer on the ballistic response and failure evolution of a thin 7075-T651 substrate. Ballistic experiments were conducted on 1.59-mm monolithic 7075-T651 targets and 2.4/1.59-mm 2024-T351/7075-T651 bilayer targets, and the results were combined with three-dimensional finite-element simulations employing material-specific constitutive and fracture models. The bilayer configuration exhibited an experimental ballistic-limit velocity of 184.8 m/s, compared with 102.4 m/s for the monolithic 7075-T651 target, corresponding to a configuration-level increase of 80.5%. This shift was accompanied by a more localized damaged region and petaling in the bilayer rear plate, linking the presence of the 2024-T351 front layer to changes in both stress-state histories and failure morphology. A knowledge-distilled artificial neural network constitutive model for finite element simulation of hot-deformed β-C titanium alloy (2026-10-27)
知识蒸馏人工神经网络本构模型用于有限元模拟热变形β-C钛合金
Tong Zhao, Youze Ma, Haisheng Xu, Xuexiong Li, Jinhu Zhang, Ming Hu, Chang Liu, Shujun Li 等 12 人
Journal of Materials Informatics
开发基于知识蒸馏的人工神经网络本构模型，用于热变形β-C钛合金的有限元模拟。
Accurate, computationally deployable constitutive models are required for reliable finite element (FE) simulations of metallic hot deformation. This study develops a knowledge-distilled artificial neural network (KD-ANN) constitutive model for β-C titanium alloy using 24 flow-stress curves measured at 800-1,050 °C and strain rates of 0.01-10 s -1 . An Extra Trees model serves as the teacher, while a compact multilayer perceptron provides a continuous constitutive mapping with explicit derivative evaluation for FE implementation. The student is trained using experimental and teacher supervision, weighted Huber loss, curve-balanced weighting and enhanced weighting in the low-strain region. Following preliminary teacher-algorithm and student-configuration screening, temperature-wise predictive performance is evaluated using curve-grouped leave-one-temperature-out validation. In each fold, all four curves at the target temperature are held out, while the fold-specific KD-ANN and modified Johnson–Cook (J–C) models are fitted using only the retained temperature conditions. Across the six held-out-temperature folds, KD-ANN achieves mean values of the coefficient of determination = 0.984, root mean square error = 11.40 MPa and mean absolute error (MAE) = 9.21 MPa, compared with 0.960, 17.12 and 12.58 MPa, respectively, for the modified J–C model. Sequential ablation and targeted control experiments show that the joint use of experimental stresses and teacher-generated targets produces the largest incremental improvement within the tested training framework. The trained student network is implemented in Abaqus through a VUHARD user subroutine. For the fold in which the lower-boundary temperature of 800 °C is held out from model fitting and validation, the FE load–displacement predictions yield a mean MAE of 0.729 kN, compared with 1.213 kN for the modified J–C model. These results demonstrate improved prediction of complete flow curves at unseen temperatures within the investigated range and support the use of KD-ANN as a constitutive input for hot-compression simulations.

This study quantifies the mechanical degradation caused by locally introduced rubber-glove contamination in stress-relieved PBF-LB/M Ti-6Al-4V. Rubber-glove fragments were inserted into defined build layers of standardized tensile specimens and into highly loaded transition regions of a topology-optimized bracket demonstrator. Twelve uncontaminated and twelve contaminated tensile specimens and nine brackets per condition were evaluated. The contaminated tensile specimens exhibited a mean ultimate strength of approx. 860 MPa compared with approx. 1200 MPa for the reference condition, corresponding to a reduction of approx. 28%. Mean elongation at break decreased from approx. 13.4% to 0.6%, representing a reduction of approx. 95%. Fracture preferentially occurred at the contamination insertion region and was associated with planar fracture features and retained spherical powder particles. The results support a predominantly defect-controlled degradation mechanism associated with locally disturbed consolidation.

ABSTRACT This study develops a cyclic thermo‐elasto‐plastic creep constitutive model within a finite‐deformation framework for structures under cyclic thermo‐mechanical loading. The A‐K‐O kinematic hardening model is combined with a Norton‐Bailey creep law, and the governing equations are discretized using a backward‐Euler return‐mapping algorithm. A consistent tangent stiffness matrix is derived to improve the convergence of nonlinear finite element analyses. Plastic dissipation is further introduced as an internal heat source in a sequentially coupled thermo‐mechanical formulation. Numerical examples show that the finite‐deformation formulation captures large plastic deformation more accurately than the small‐deformation assumption, that creep during load holding promotes ratcheting and stress redistribution, and that plastic dissipation under rapid cyclic loading produces substantial temperature rise. The proposed framework provides a stable numerical basis for evaluating deformation and heat generation in components under cyclic thermo‐mechanical loading.

Reliable identification of damage-induced property changes in formed metal sheets remains challenging because plastic deformation alters the mechanical response through several coupled mechanisms. Moreover, pronounced ductile damage evolution occurs predominantly under inhomogeneous states of deformation, e.g., strain localization, which compromises the characterization of potential effects of damage on macroscopic mechanical properties, e.g., elastic stiffness. In this study, damage in dual-phase steel DP800 sheets is isolated from other concurrent mechanisms using a novel “sheet extrusion” process. The flat, finitely-strained sheets exhibit large regions with homogeneous properties, enabling subsequent characterization of damage-induced property changes. Damage in the form of microscopic voids is then characterized by large-area scanning electron microscopy (SEM) measurements, and damage-induced property changes are determined by subsequent characterization tests on specimens extracted from the extruded sheets.
