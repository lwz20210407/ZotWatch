Temperature Dependence of Ductile Fracture in Structural Low-Carbon Steel (2026-11-07)
结构低碳钢延性断裂的温度依赖性
Andronikos Skiadopoulos, Christopher Smith, Amit M. Kanvinde, Gregory G. Deierlein
Journal of Structural Engineering
针对A572 Gr. 50低碳钢，建立温度依赖的延性断裂模型，揭示高温下断裂应变系统性降低的现象。
Abstract This study presents a temperature-dependent fracture model for predicting ductile crack initiation in low-carbon structural steel subjected to elevated temperatures. The continuum damage mechanics-based model is developed using data from twenty-five coupon tests on A572 Gr. 50 (345 MPa) steel on specimens with a wide range of stress triaxialities and Lode angle parameters over temperatures from 20°C to 700°C. Complementary finite element simulations replicate the stress–strain response up to fracture, while micromechanical void cell simulations provide insight into temperature-driven damage mechanisms. The experimental results show a systematic decrease in fracture strain with increasing temperature up to 600°C, attributed to diminished strain hardening leading to accelerated localization of plastic deformation at the microvoid scale. Void cell simulations corroborate these trends, demonstrating increased void growth rate and reduced localization strains at elevated temperatures. Based on these findings, the stress-weighted ductile fracture model (SWDFM) is extended to incorporate temperature dependence under varying stress triaxiality and Lode angle. The model is calibrated using the new experimental data, validated across multiple specimen geometries and stress states, and assessed for robustness through cross-validation.

Substructure–enabled dynamic strain aging and κ-carbide nano-precipitation in a high-SFE Fe–Mn–Al–C lightweight steel (2026-11-04)
高SFE Fe–Mn–Al–C轻质钢中亚结构诱导的动态应变时效与κ-碳化物纳米析出
Hamid Reza Abedi, Abbas Zarei Hanzaki, Ali Moshiri, A.M. Jamili
Materials & Design
揭示高SFE轻质钢中亚结构诱导的动态应变时效机制及其与κ-碳化物析出的关联。
Classical theories predict that dynamic-strain-aging (DSA) should be strongly suppressed in high stacking-fault-energy (SFE) Fe–Mn–Al–C steels, because narrow, temperature-sensitive stacking-fault widths limit solute–dislocation interaction times. Contrary to these expectations, we demonstrate that a high-SFE lightweight steel exhibits pronounced DSA from 100-to-500 °C, a regime traditionally considered unlikely. Despite serrated-flow and negative strain-rate-sensitivity, the alloy retains remarkably stable ductility and work-hardening capability, revealing a fundamentally non-classical DSA response that is not governed by conventional stacking-fault-mediated solute–dislocation interactions. Electron-backscatter-diffraction shows rapid formation of a boundary-rich substructure consisting of dense low-angle-boundaries and refined subgrains that concentrate mobile dislocations at interfaces. This deformation-generated-network acts as a fast-diffusion scaffold that enables stress-assisted pipe and boundary diffusion of interstitial and substitutional solutes, activating a DSA mechanism independent of stacking-fault-mediated interactions. Transmission electron microscopy and selected-area diffraction reveal ordering-like nanoscale domains consistent with deformation-assisted short-range-ordering near 300 °C and a transition to κ-carbide(L′1 2 ) nanoprecipitation above 500 °C, indicating that deformation-induced solute redistribution associated with the DSA regime may facilitate subsequent κ-phase formation at higher temperatures. These findings uncover a previously unreported substructure-assisted DSA mechanism in high-SFE steels, explain the coexistence of strong DSA and sustained ductility, and establish a mechanistic link between warm-temperature DSA, dynamic ordering, and κ-carbide precipitation.

综述板料弯曲过程中的应变局域化、损伤演化与断裂机制，分析不同材料的响应差异。
Sheet-metal bending produces pronounced through-thickness strain gradients and localized deformation. The resulting damage and fracture depend on microstructure, specimen geometry, loading path, and failure criterion. This review examines bending configurations, stress and strain distributions, neutral-layer evolution, bendability measures, and multiscale characterization methods, and compares damage behavior in steel, aluminum, copper, magnesium, and titanium alloys. It then considers damage initiation and crack propagation in relation to surface roughening and strain localization, slip and shear bands, second phases and interfacial damage, and twinning and tension–compression asymmetry. Most studies describe bending damage in different metals through related stages of heterogeneous microscale deformation, strain localization, damage initiation, and crack propagation. Microstructural heterogeneity and initial defects govern local deformation compatibility, while geometry and processing conditions affect strain gradients, constraint, and damage accumulation. Limit bend angle, minimum bend radius, and load-drop point are apparent measures determined jointly by the material, specimen, and process; none directly measures intrinsic fracture resistance. Bendability should therefore be evaluated using local strain fields, crack evidence, and microstructural evolution together. The review provides guidance for material selection, bending-test design, model calibration, and the definition of safe processing windows, while identifying in situ multiscale characterization, microstructure-informed damage modeling, AI-assisted damage prediction, and standardized bendability assessment as priorities for future research.

To address the insufficient understanding of the surface plastic deformation mechanism of Ti-6Al-4V alloy during ultrasonic surface rolling processing (USRP), this study adopts an approach combining finite element (FE) simulation and molecular dynamics (MD) analysis. The effects of static load, ultrasonic vibration frequency, and ultrasonic amplitude on residual stress and surface plastic deformation are investigated, and the dislocation evolution and local deformation response under ultrasonic vibration are analyzed at the atomic scale. A three-dimensional explicit dynamic FE model based on the Johnson-Cook constitutive law is established to examine the variations of surface residual stress, equivalent plastic strain, and plastic displacement under different static loads, ultrasonic vibration frequencies, and ultrasonic amplitudes, and the validity of the FE model is verified against experimentally measured residual stress results. The results show that static load is the dominant factor governing the degree of macroscopic surface plastic deformation. Within the investigated static-load range of 100–400 N, the magnitude of the peak residual compressive stress increases overall from 685.11 MPa to 722.09 MPa, although the variation is not strictly monotonic, while the maximum PEEQ increases from 0.12481 to 0.38641 and the affected depths increase markedly. These observations suggest that periodic excitation modifies local defect evolution and indentation response within the simplified atomic model, providing complementary insights into atomic-scale plastic deformation. This study can provide a reference for process optimization and surface strengthening mechanism research of Ti-6Al-4V alloy during USRP.
